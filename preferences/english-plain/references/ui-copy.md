# UI copy patterns

Follow the platform's or design system's standard labels where they exist, such as "OK" in an iOS alert. Where you have a choice, use the patterns below.

## Patterns

- Buttons and calls to action: use a verb that names the result. "Save document", "Send application". Prefer these to "Submit" or "Process".
- Empty states: explain the situation and offer a next step. "Nothing here yet. Start by adding your first item."
- Success messages: short, warm, and concrete. "Your profile is updated." Not "Operation completed successfully."
- Error messages: say what happened and what to do next, and keep the code. "We couldn't load your orders. Try again in a few minutes. (Code 503)" Not "Error 503."
- Field errors: name the field and show the fix. "Enter an email address like name@example.com." Not "Invalid input."
- Placeholders: use them only for format examples, such as "name@example.com". When a placeholder is doing the job of a label, recommend a visible label to the user.
- Tooltips and helper text: one short sentence that answers "Why do I need to fill this in?" or "What goes here?"
- Destructive confirmations: state the consequence. "Delete account? Your data will be erased and can't be recovered." Not "Are you sure?" Confirm in the code what the action really does before you write the consequence.
- Onboarding: invite when the step is optional. "Let's start by setting up your profile." When a step is required, say so plainly.
- Loading and waiting: say what is happening. Give a duration only when the product knows it.
- Consent and permissions: explain what you ask for and why, in one plain sentence, before the system prompt appears.

## Fit the space

When the plain version does not fit, shorten the wording before you drop meaning. If it still does not fit, give the user the shortest accurate version and say that the space is too small.

## Natural sentence patterns

Write the way a person would say it.

- Prefer: "You can change your password at any time."
- Avoid: "Password changes can be performed at any time by the user."

- Prefer: "Fill in all required fields."
- Avoid: "Please ensure that all fields designated as mandatory have been completed."

- Prefer: "We couldn't send it. Please try again."
- Avoid: "An unexpected system error has occurred. Kindly repeat the process."

- Prefer: "Save changes?"
- Avoid: "Are you sure you wish to save the modifications that have been made?"

Leave out formal words such as "kindly", "whereby", "herein", and "aforementioned".
